```markdown
Many patients treat herbal teas, capsules, and concentrated botanical extracts as gentle food products rather than pharmacologically active compounds. In practice, several widely used herbs induce or inhibit cytochrome P450 enzymes (such as CYP3A4, CYP2C9, and CYP1A2), P-glycoprotein transport, or platelet aggregation at clinically relevant doses.

Below are five high-priority herb–medication checks documented across Health Canada monographs, European Medicines Agency (EMA HMPC) assessments, NIH NCCIH summaries, and peer-reviewed literature — and how patients and clinicians can screen them quickly for free at HerbAlly (https://herbally.app).

### 1. St. John's Wort (_Hypericum perforatum_) + Antidepressants or CYP3A4 Substrates

St. John's wort is a potent inducer of CYP3A4, CYP2C9, and P-glycoprotein and increases synaptic serotonin. Combining it with SSRIs or SNRIs (such as sertraline) risks serotonin toxicity, while enzyme induction can markedly reduce blood levels of immunosuppressants, oral contraceptives, digoxin, and direct oral anticoagulants.

- Review pairs: https://herbally.app/interactions/st-johns-wort

### 2. Garlic, Ginkgo, Turmeric, or High-Dose Ginger + Anticoagulants / Antiplatelets (Warfarin, Aspirin, Apixaban, Clopidogrel)

Botanicals with antiplatelet or anticoagulant activity can create additive bleeding risk when taken alongside warfarin or antiplatelet therapy — particularly concentrated extracts rather than culinary pinch amounts.

- Reverse lookup by medication: https://herbally.app/medications/warfarin

### 3. Pre-Surgery Botanical Washouts (7 to 14 Days Before Anaesthesia)

American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) guidance and Health Canada monographs advise stopping herbs that affect bleeding risk, blood glucose, sedation, or cardiovascular tone well ahead of elective surgery — commonly 7 to 14 days prior, depending on the botanical and surgical team's protocol.

- Printable washout checklist: https://herbally.app/surgery-planner

### 4. Licorice Root (_Glycyrrhiza glabra_) + Corticosteroids, Diuretics, or Antihypertensives

Glycyrrhizin in non-deglycyrrhizinated licorice inhibits 11-beta-hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase type 2, promoting sodium retention and potassium loss. Alongside loop or thiazide diuretics, digoxin, or systemic corticosteroids, hypokalaemia and hypertension risks rise significantly.

- Reverse lookup by class or drug: https://herbally.app/medications

### 5. Reading Multi-Ingredient Natural Health Products via Health Canada's NPN

A single supplement bottle labeled "stress relief" or "joint support" often blends four to eight herbs under trademarked names. On Canadian-licensed products, the 8-digit Natural Product Number (NPN) ties directly to the active botanical ingredients and official cautions in Health Canada's LNHPD.

- Live decoder: https://herbally.app/npn-checker
```
